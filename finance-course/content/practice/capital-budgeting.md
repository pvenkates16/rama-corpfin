Q: A project costs $1,000 today and pays $600 at the end of each of the next two years. At a discount rate of 10%, what is its NPV?
A: $$NPV = -1000 + \frac{600}{1.10} + \frac{600}{1.10^2} \approx 41.32$$
The NPV is positive, so the project creates value and should be accepted.

Q: Why can IRR and NPV rank mutually exclusive projects differently?
A: IRR ignores project scale and implicitly assumes cash flows are reinvested at the IRR itself, while NPV assumes reinvestment at the cost of capital. When projects differ in size or timing, the rankings can conflict. Use NPV.
